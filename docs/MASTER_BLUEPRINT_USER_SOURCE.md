🟢 Tier 1: Matter & Atomic Frameworks (Grade 9 Baseline - Easiest)\
\
Focuses on qualitative observation, basic physical manipulation, and particle sorting rules.\
• 1. Mixtures Separation & Compound Formation Dynamics:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A sandbox containing scattered iron filings and sulfur powder. Students drag a virtual magnet over the plate to physically separate the magnetic iron (Physical Mixture). They then click a Bunsen burner to heat the mixture; a localized thermal animation forces iron and sulfur atoms to clash, bond, and transform into non-magnetic, solid black Iron Sulphide (FeS) crystals.\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Visually distinguish between physical mixtures and chemical compounds.\
• 2. Interactive 3D Periodic Table Matrix:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A glossy 3D Periodic Table dashboard. Clicking any element expands a rotating orbital model. Toggling property sliders maps a colorful heatmap overlay across rows and groups to visually illustrate how metallic character shifts and how atomic radius physically contracts from left to right.\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Master the structural organization of elements and primary periodic variations.\
• 3. The Bohr-Rutherford Shell Constructor:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Drag-and-drop particle baskets containing protons, neutrons, and electrons. As students drop particles onto a blank center target, the software automatically snaps electrons into planetary, concentric orbits following strict K, L, M, N layer limits (2, 8, 8 configuration rules).\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Calculate atomic number, mass number, and standard electronic configurations.\
• 4. Valence Electrostatic & Ionization Factory:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Students deploy a virtual "laser beam" tool to blast valence electrons out of metal shells (shrinking the atom to yield a stable Cation like Na⁺) or force stray electrons into a non-metal shell to complete an octet ring (expanding the atom to yield an Anion like Cl⁻).\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Understand valence configurations, stable octet configurations, and net ionic charges.\
• 5. Law of Conservation of Mass Validation Scale:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A closed dual-chamber conical flask filled with colorless Barium Chloride and Sodium Sulphate solutions sits on a digital scale reading to ±0.01g. Students click to tip the flask over; a 3D sub-window animates a rapid double-displacement precipitation reaction forming solid white clouds while the digital weight readout on the scale remains completely frozen.\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Prove the Law of Conservation of Mass empirically through fixed gravimetric balances.\

🟡 Tier 2: Bonding & Structural Reactions (Grade 10 - Medium)\

Introduces atomic interactions, chemical bonding classes, and foundational organic naming logic.\
• 6. Ionic Bonding & 3D Crystal Lattice Sandbox:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Side-by-side models of Na and Cl. The student must click and drag the lone valence electron from Na's shell across the space into Cl's open slot. &#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Students select two non-metals (e.g., two Oxygen atoms). They must drag separate valence electrons into a central "Shared Zone". • 8. The Four Fundamental Chemical Reaction Types:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A laboratory bench holding various test materials. &#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Identify and systematically classify Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, and Double Displacement reactions.\

&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Dropper bottles containing litmus paper, universal indicator fluid, and digital pH probes. &#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Read the pH scale log scale and trace chemical properties via indicator arrays.\
• 10. Aliphatic Hydrocarbons & Real-Time IUPAC Naming:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: An interactive 2D structure editor canvas. Students click to chain Carbon atoms using single, double, or triple bond nodes and satisfy remaining open links with Hydrogen atoms. &#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Master basic organic naming conventions, carbon tetravalency, and homologous series.\

🟠 Tier 3: Quantitative Metering & Thermodynamics (Grade 11 - Hard)\
\
Introduces stoichiometric logic, ideal gas physics, system stress simulations, and quantum mechanics.\
• 11. Stoichiometric Equation Balancer & Balance Beam:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: An unbalanced skeletal reaction (e.g., Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃) sits over a balance scale. Students toggle coefficients, causing atomic weights to drop onto left or right plates. The software hard-locks the subscript digits; trying to change a subscript throws an explicit error: ❌ Modifying subscripts changes the core identity of the substance!\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Perform standard chemical reaction accounting and molar balance calculations.\
• 12. Solution Preparation & Molarity Calibration:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Students select a custom dry reagent powder, evaluate its molecular mass, weigh out precise grams on a digital scale, and funnel the solute into a volumetric flask to fill it with distilled water to the meniscus ring to calculate target solution concentrations (e.g., 1.0 M).\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Connect the mass concept to molar concentration calculation sheets.\
• 13. Ideal Gas Laws Piston Engine:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A sealed cylinder packed with thousands of bouncing particle spheres. Students slide dashboard targets to alter Volume, Pressure, or Temperature. Forcing volume down rapidly makes particles collide violently with the cylinder walls, making the pressure dial jump and plotting exact real-time curves for Boyle’s and Charles’s laws (PV=nRT).\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Mathematically track gas variable distributions and kinetic behaviors.\
• 14. Quantum Mechanics 3D Space Orbitals:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: Abandons classical rings. The software renders glowing, translucent 3D electron cloud shapes (s spheres, p dumb-bells, d cross-clovers). Students enter inputs for principal and angular quantum numbers (\\(n, l, m_l\\)) to rotate the probability geometries 360 degrees.\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Transition from planetary atomic pathways to modern quantum density concepts.\
• 15. Aufbau Energy Sequence Electronic Filler:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A tiered quantum ladder showing empty subshell boxes: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p. Students drag spin arrows into the boxes. The software strictly validates Hund’s Rule (fill singly first) and the Pauli Exclusion Principle (paired electrons must point in opposite directions). Mismatched spin inputs pop right back out.\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Master the electronic configurations of multi-electron ground states.\
• 16. Insulated Bomb Calorimetry & Enthalpy Calculation:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: An insulated reaction container fitted with a real-time stirring hotkey and an digital micro-thermometer. Students mix HCl and NaOH, then must rapidly tap the stir key to distribute heat evenly. The system tracks a smooth temperature curve to solve for enthalpy via:\
\\(q=m\cdot c\cdot \Delta T\\)\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Experimentally quantify exothermic heat releases and enthalpy configurations.\
• 17. Le Chatelier's Reversible Equilibrium Matrix:\
&#x9;• The Visual Animation & UI: A chemical system containing Iron(III) Nitrate and Potassium Thiocyanate in dynamic equilibrium. Adding random reagents changes reactant levels or alters temperature. The background solver applies the law of mass action (\\(K\_{c}\\)) to recalculate ion tracking, shifting the fluid opacity across a smooth color spectrum (pale yellow to deep blood-red).\
&#x9;• Core Learning Objective: Visualize equilibrium adjustments under applied environmental stresses.\
